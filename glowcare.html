GlowCare Complaint Care Assistant.

A prompt-engineered intake chatbot for a direct-to-consumer cosmetics brand. Where Artifact 01 explains AI, this one is AI at work: a single carefully constrained system prompt turns a general-purpose LLM into a specialist that collects complete complaint details with empathy — and hands support teams a clean, structured ticket every time.

AIML-500 · AI Lab System prompt design Chatbase deployment HTML/JS prototype Node/Express + Anthropic API

The project, briefly

Built for the AIML-500 AI Lab "Build a Bot" activity, then extended beyond the assignment into a deployable product.

Context

GlowCare is a D2C skincare & makeup brand. Complaints arrive as unstructured chat — missing order numbers, vague product references, mixed emotions — forcing agents to play twenty questions before they can even open a ticket.

Challenge

Make one LLM reliably do one narrow job: gather every required field (product, order #, issue, evidence) without sounding like a form — and refuse to drift into refunds, medical advice, or off-topic chat.

Solution

A role-scoped system prompt with an explicit collection checklist, empathy-first response rules, safety escalation triggers, and a mandatory structured JSON summary — deployed on Chatbase and rebuilt as a key-safe Node/Express app.

1 · Empathize & capture the issue

The prompt requires acknowledging frustration before asking anything. Safety keywords (rash, burn, allergic) are flagged immediately.

2 · Identify the product

One question at a time — the prompt forbids stacking questions, which is what makes bots feel like forms.

3 · Collect the order number

Validates the GC-XXXXX pattern; politely re-asks (once) if the format looks off, then proceeds rather than blocking.

4 · Ask for evidence

Photos are requested, never demanded — the prompt treats them as optional and records availability either way.

5 · Structured summary & routing

Every conversation ends in the same machine-readable JSON: category, priority, and an escalation flag downstream systems can route on.

The system prompt, annotated

The entire "application logic" lives in natural language. Every behavior in the demo above traces to a line below.

 system_prompt.txt
ROLE: You are the Complaint Care Assistant for GlowCare,
a direct-to-consumer cosmetics brand (skincare and makeup).
Your only job is to collect complete product complaint
details empathetically and produce a structured summary.

TONE: Warm, professional, concise. Always acknowledge
the customer's frustration before asking for information.
Ask exactly ONE question per message.

COLLECT (in order):
1. What happened (the complaint itself)
2. Product name / shade / size
3. Order number (format GC-XXXXX; if it doesn't match,
   ask once more, then continue without blocking)
4. When the issue was noticed
5. Whether the customer can share a photo (optional)

SAFETY: If the customer mentions a skin reaction,
rash, burning, or allergy — flag priority HIGH, advise
discontinuing use, and mark the case for escalation to
the Product Safety team. Never give medical advice.

GUARDRAILS: Do not promise refunds, replacements, or
timelines. Do not discuss topics outside complaint intake.
If asked, explain a specialist will follow up within
2 business days.

OUTPUT: When all fields are gathered, thank the customer
and emit a JSON summary: {category, product, order_id,
description, reported_when, photo_available, priority,
escalate, sentiment}.
Role scoping"Your only job" — the single most effective jailbreak- and drift-resistance line. The bot declines everything outside intake.
Interaction contractOne question per message keeps completion rates high; empathy-first ordering is a rule, not a vibe.
Graceful validationRe-ask once, then proceed — availability beats perfection in intake flows.
Safety & escalationDeterministic triggers for health-related complaints, with an explicit "no medical advice" boundary.
Structured outputEnding every chat in identical JSON is what makes an LLM composable with routers, dashboards, and CRMs.
