Data Fusion in Formula 1 Racing

Correlating grid position, driver performance, and constructor dominance across six F1 seasons — a hands-on demonstration of statistical reasoning and data-fusion methodology applied outside a business dataset, to show the same skill set transfers to any domain.

The question

Does qualifying position actually predict race outcome, or is Formula 1 as unpredictable as fans like to believe? Five data dimensions — grid position, finishing position, championship points, constructor, and season — were fused from 2,559 race entries across 34 circuits (2,244 valid entries after removing DNFs/DSQs) to find out.

Beyond the headline number

Data fusion means no single variable tells the whole story. Layering constructor identity onto the grid/finish relationship revealed that dominance itself is not static: Red Bull Racing peaked in 2023 (790 points, 21 wins), then McLaren and Ferrari closed the gap through 2024 — meaning the same grid slot was worth measurably more or less depending on the season and the car under the driver. At the individual level, Max Verstappen accumulated 2,242.5 championship points across 117 classified finishes over the period — a 24.4% lead over the second-ranked driver — which the correlation alone doesn't explain without also accounting for reliability and team strategy.

"No single variable fully explains performance — the intersection of mechanical reliability, driver skill, qualifying pace, and strategic decision-making creates the holistic competitive landscape."
